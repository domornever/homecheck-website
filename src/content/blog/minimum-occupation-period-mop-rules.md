# Minimum Occupation Period (MOP) Rules: The Complete HDB Resale Flat Guide

If you're eyeing a sale, a bigger rental income, or an upgrade to a larger place, it's easy to assume the Minimum Occupation Period is just a countdown you wait out — five years, done, move on. It isn't that simple. The flat type you bought — Standard, Plus, or Prime — shapes not just how long you wait, but what you're allowed to do with the flat once the waiting is over.

Minimum Occupation Period MOP rules require HDB flat owners to physically occupy their flat for five years before selling, renting, or buying private property. Standard flats carry this five-year MOP, while newer Plus and Prime flats require ten years and permanently bar whole-flat rental even after MOP ends.

This guide walks through what MOP actually covers, how long it runs for each flat type, and what you can and can't do while you're serving it. It also covers the part most guides skip: the permanent gap in rental rights between Standard flats and their Plus and Prime counterparts — a distinction that outlasts MOP itself. From there, we cover exemptions for life events like divorce or death, how to check your own MOP completion date, and where BTO applications fit in if you're still serving MOP on your current flat.

## 1. What Is the Minimum Occupation Period (MOP)?

The Minimum Occupation Period (MOP) is the minimum length of time an HDB flat owner must physically live in the flat before they can sell it, rent out the whole unit, or buy private residential property. Physically live is the operative phrase. Registering the flat as your address on paper doesn't satisfy MOP; [HDB's own eligibility framing](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/managing-my-home/selling-a-flat/eligibility) treats it as a residence requirement, not a paperwork one.

Getting a handle on Minimum Occupation Period MOP rules early saves a lot of confusion later, especially once flat type enters the picture.

MOP applies across HDB flat types — BTO flats, resale flats, and Executive Condominiums (ECs) all carry some form of it — though, as the next section covers, the exact duration and what happens once it ends differ by flat type. If you're specifically tracking Resale flat Minimum Occupation Period timelines rather than a BTO's, the same duration split still applies — it's the flat type classification that matters, not whether you bought new or resale.

It's worth separating MOP from the other conditions that govern whether you can sell a resale flat at all. MOP answers *when* you can sell, rent, or [buy private property](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/managing-my-home/home-ownership/acquiring-private-property). Separately, HDB's Ethnic Integration Policy (EIP) and Singapore Permanent Resident (SPR) quota rules answer *whether* a buyer is eligible to purchase your specific flat, based on the ethnic and citizenship mix already in that block or neighbourhood. Both sit alongside each other as resale-eligibility conditions, but they're answering different questions.

## 2. MOP Duration by Flat Type: Standard, Plus, Prime, and More

Under [HDB's Standard, Plus, and Prime flat classification](https://www.mynicehome.gov.sg/get-started/hdb-flat-types-classification-guide/) — which applies to flats launched from October 2024 onward — MOP length and what happens after it ends both vary by flat type. This distinction is the foundation for the rental-rights gap covered in the next section, so it's worth understanding clearly before assuming your MOP works the same way a friend's or family member's does. The Minimum Occupation Period MOP rules that apply to a friend's Standard flat may look nothing like the ones that apply to your own Plus or Prime flat.

Flats launched before October 2024 don't fall under this three-tier system. They're "unclassified" and follow the resale-eligibility conditions that applied when they were launched, rather than the newer Standard, Plus, and Prime framework.

### Standard Flats: 5-Year MOP

Standard BTO and resale flats carry the MOP most buyers are already familiar with: five years. Once that period is served, there's no subsidy clawback when you resell — you keep the full resale value. And once MOP is served, Standard flat owners are free to rent out the whole flat, a right that, as the next section covers, doesn't extend to Plus and Prime flats in the same way.

### Plus and Prime Flats: 10-Year MOP

Plus and Prime flats — HDB's newer, more centrally located flat categories — carry a ten-year MOP, double the Standard-flat duration. Subsidy recovery applies on resale for both flat types, clawing back a portion of the housing subsidy when the flat is eventually sold.

But the bigger difference isn't the extra five years of waiting. It's what happens afterward — which is exactly what the next section covers.

### Executive Condominiums (ECs) and SERS Flats

Executive Condominium (EC) MOP rules have been subject to recent government revisions, and the applicable MOP and full-privatisation timeline — the point at which an EC can be sold to any buyer, including foreigners — can depend on your EC's Government Land Sales (GLS) tender date. Because these terms vary by launch date and are subject to change, confirm your EC's exact MOP and full-privatisation timeline directly with [HDB](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/buying-a-flat/executive-condominiums/conditions-after-buying-an-ec) rather than assuming a fixed figure. Owners and their spouses are bound by the same restriction during the MOP window — neither can acquire private residential property until it's served.

Two related points come up often in property guides but are worth confirming with HDB directly rather than treating as settled: whether a resale EC — one where an earlier owner already served the MOP — carries no fresh MOP requirement for the buyer who purchases it on resale, and whether SERS replacement-flat owners and 1-room resale flat buyers now serve the same standard MOP as other buyers. Both points check out against the general property-guide consensus, but neither is something HDB's own EC or SERS-specific pages could confirm for this guide. If either scenario applies to you, confirm directly with HDB rather than relying on secondary sources.

## 3. Rental Rules After MOP by Flat Type

<!-- info-gain anchor: angle 1 -->

Here's the distinction that most MOP guides gloss over: the difference between flat types isn't just about how long you wait. It's about what you're permanently allowed to do with the flat once you're done waiting.

Once a Standard flat owner serves the five-year MOP, they can rent out the entire flat to eligible tenants — Singapore Citizens or Permanent Residents — without any further restriction tied to their flat's classification. For Plus and Prime flats, that door never opens. Whole-flat rental stays off the table permanently, even after the full ten-year MOP is served. It isn't a temporary restriction tied to the MOP clock winding down — it's a structural condition of owning a Plus or Prime flat in the first place.

Bedroom rental works differently and sits on its own track. [Renting out a spare room to an eligible tenant](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/managing-my-home/home-ownership/renting-out-a-flat-or-bedrooms) runs on a separate eligibility track from whole-flat rental — it isn't gated by a flat's Standard, Plus, or Prime classification the same way whole-flat rental is, though it carries its own separate conditions.

If long-term rental income is part of your plan when you buy, this is worth weighing at purchase time, not just when your MOP clock is about to run out. A Standard flat and a Plus or Prime flat can carry a similar price tag today and a very different rental future.

## 4. What You Can't Do During MOP

During MOP, the restrictions themselves are straightforward, even if the reasoning behind them — physical residence, not paperwork — sometimes isn't. You can't sell the flat on the open market until MOP is fully served.

You also can't rent out the whole flat. Only bedroom rental to eligible tenants is allowed while you're serving MOP, and you — the owner — need to remain in occupation yourself; you can't rent the whole place out and live elsewhere.

Neither you nor your spouse can acquire private residential property until MOP is fully served. This applies to both of you together, not just the flat owner named on the application.

And as covered earlier, leaving the flat vacant or genuinely unoccupied during MOP isn't something you can quietly work around — physical residence is what HDB's framing of MOP is actually built around, not just what address is on file.

## 5. MOP Exemptions: Divorce, Death, and Other Life Events

MOP isn't absolute — [HDB does allow for case-by-case exceptions](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/managing-my-home/home-ownership/change-of-flat-owners-or-occupiers/retain-flat-following-life-events) when specific life events happen before it's served. None of these are automatic waivers, though; each one requires a direct application to HDB with supporting documentation.

If you go through a divorce, HDB allows you to retain the flat under alternative arrangements — with or without children. Alternatively, once MOP is met, you can sell the flat on the open market.

Death of an owner is handled differently depending on how the flat was owned. Under joint ownership, the deceased owner's share transfers automatically to the surviving owner or owners. Under sole or co-ownership, the interest is distributed according to the owner's will, or under the Intestate Succession Act if there's no will.

Loss of citizenship or Singapore PR status is its own trigger. It prompts an HDB eligibility review of the household's right to retain the flat at all — which, depending on the outcome, may require the flat to be sold or surrendered.

None of these outcomes are guaranteed. Treat them as case-by-case pathways to raise directly with HDB, not as automatic exits from MOP.

## 6. How to Check Your MOP Completion Date

Owners can check their MOP completion status by logging in to My HDBPage with Singpass and checking their flat details. HDB updates the portal's menu structure from time to time, so rather than following a fixed click-path, look for your flat's MOP information within the flat-details section, or check with HDB directly if you can't locate it.

One common point of confusion is when the MOP clock actually starts. Exactly when it starts and ends can vary by flat type and purchase method. Confirm your own start and end dates directly through My HDBPage rather than working from a general rule of thumb.

Either way, knowing your exact MOP end date matters more than it might seem. It's what lets you time a sale, a whole-flat rental, or a private-property purchase correctly — get the date wrong and you risk running into the restrictions covered above before you're actually clear of them. Because Minimum Occupation Period MOP rules hinge on an exact start date rather than an approximate one, it's worth confirming yours early rather than only checking when you're ready to sell.

## 7. Buying a New Flat or BTO While Serving MOP

If you're still serving MOP on your current flat, a natural question comes up: does that stop you from applying for a new BTO flat? [Check directly with HDB](https://www.mnd.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-matters/q-as/view/written-answer-by-ministry-of-national-development-on-proposal-to-allow-existing-hdb-flat-owners-whose-hdb-flats-are-still-within-the-minimum-occupation-period-to-apply-for-new-bto-flats) — HDB's prevailing rules for flat owners still within MOP may include exceptions for specific circumstances, so confirm your own position before assuming either way.

Once MOP is served, the picture changes — but MOP completion on its own still doesn't automatically clear you for a new BTO application. [Separate BTO purchase-eligibility conditions](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/buying-a-flat/bto-sbf-and-open-booking-of-flats/conditions-after-buying-a-new-flat) — including disposal of any existing flat or private property, among other requirements — apply on top of MOP completion. Don't assume the two are linked without checking; MOP and BTO purchase-eligibility are, at minimum, separate sets of rules answering separate questions.

What is confirmed: if you're a second-timer buying a subsidised flat, the resale levy applies. It reduces the housing subsidy on your new flat, and it's a standing condition of buying again — separate from whatever your MOP status is on your current place.

One recent change is also worth knowing about, even though it doesn't touch MOP directly. As of 27 July 2026, HDB removed the 15-month wait-out period specifically for private-property owners (and ex-owners) buying a non-subsidised HDB resale flat without an HDB housing loan. That's a purchase-side rule change, not a change to a seller's own MOP obligation — the two shouldn't be conflated. Rules for other buyer groups — such as buyers of subsidised flats via BTO, Sale of Balance Flats, or grant-assisted resale — are governed by separate wait-out conditions; [confirm the current terms with HDB](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/removal-of-the-15-month-wait-out-period-for-private-residential-property-owners) before relying on this for your own purchase timeline, since this change specifically targeted private-property owners buying non-subsidised resale flats.

## 8. FAQ

### What is the minimum occupancy period for MOP?

Minimum Occupation Period MOP rules set this figure at five years for Standard HDB flats — the category most BTO and resale flats fall into. Plus and Prime flats, HDB's newer, more centrally located categories, carry a longer ten-year MOP instead. During this period, the flat owner must physically live in the flat before it can be sold, rented out in full, or before the household can buy private residential property.

### Can I BTO again after MOP?

Not automatically. Completing MOP on your current flat clears one condition, but it isn't the only one — separate BTO purchase-eligibility rules, including disposal of any existing flat or private property, apply on top of it. If you're a second-timer, the resale levy also applies. Confirm your household's full eligibility with HDB, via an HFE letter application, rather than assuming MOP completion is the whole picture.

### How do I check my MOP period?

Through My HDBPage, using your Singpass login — check your flat details for the MOP completion date. If your MOP end date matters for a near-term decision — selling, renting, or buying — confirm it directly in the portal rather than working it out from memory.

### Can I apply for BTO before MOP?

HDB's prevailing rules generally restrict flat owners from applying for a new flat while still serving MOP on their current one, though case-by-case exceptions may apply for specific circumstances. Confirm your own position directly with HDB rather than assuming either way before you apply.

## Conclusion

Minimum Occupation Period MOP rules aren't one uniform five-year countdown that works the same way for every HDB flat. How long you wait — five years or ten — and, more importantly, what you can do with the flat once you're done waiting, both trace back to whether you bought a Standard, Plus, or Prime flat. That second part is the one buyers tend to miss: a Plus or Prime flat's whole-flat rental restriction doesn't expire with MOP. It's permanent.
