# HDB Eligibility by Citizenship Status: SC, PR, and Non-Citizen Buyer Rules

## Introduction

Before you think about flat type, budget, or which estate you'd like to live in, one question decides whether you can buy an HDB flat at all: who's in your household, and what's their citizenship status. Get this wrong and everything downstream — income ceiling checks, grant applications, even browsing listings — is moot. There's no point shopping seriously for a flat you can't legally buy. It's the first gate, and it trips up more buyers than you'd expect, especially households with a mix of citizens, Permanent Residents, and non-citizen family members.

Citizenship status HDB eligibility hinges on the household having one Singapore Citizen, or two Permanent Residents each holding PR status for three years. Resale purchases face two further checks: the Ethnic Integration Policy, which limits buyers by ethnic group, and an SPR quota capping non-Malaysian Permanent Resident households at 8% per block.

This article walks through the three household configurations that qualify — a Singapore Citizen (SC) household, an SC plus Singapore Permanent Resident (SPR) household, and an SC plus non-citizen spouse — along with the often-overlooked 3-year PR tenure rule and the Ethnic Integration Policy and SPR quota gates that apply on resale purchases even after your household clears citizenship eligibility. Once you know where you stand, the practical next step is working through the [HDB Eligibility (HFE) letter](https://hometheory.sg/blog/hdb-flat-eligibility-hfe-letter-application/) process, which confirms your eligibility formally before you shortlist a flat.

## 1. Citizenship Eligibility Overview

Citizenship status HDB eligibility comes down to one of three household shapes: a Singapore Citizen (SC) household, an SC plus Singapore Permanent Resident (SPR) household, or an SC plus a non-citizen spouse or family member buying under a named scheme. There's no fourth option — households that don't have a Singapore Citizen or an eligible Permanent Resident combination don't fit any of these three configurations.

This citizenship gate is the same whether you're eyeing a BTO launch, a resale flat, or an Executive Condominium (EC) — but which specific schemes and grants you can use differs by household composition. If you're a single Singapore Citizen, the mechanics differ enough from a couple's that they get their own treatment in our guide to the Single Singapore Citizen Scheme.

The three configurations below each carry different scheme access, so it's worth knowing which one your household falls into before you start comparing flat types.

### Singapore Citizen Households

Singapore Citizen HDB scheme eligibility is the most flexible of the three configurations: SC-only households — a married couple, a family nucleus, or an eligible single aged 35 and above — qualify for the broadest range of schemes. That means BTO, resale, EC, and the widest grant access available under any HDB scheme.

Single-citizen buyers do face additional age and marital-status conditions that this article doesn't cover in depth. If that's your situation, our Single Singapore Citizen Scheme guide walks through the specific eligibility criteria.

### Singapore Citizen + Permanent Resident Households

For an SC plus SPR household, the household must include one Singapore Citizen plus at least one SPR family-nucleus member who has held PR status for three years or more — we cover that tenure rule in detail further down this page.

PR-inclusive households can buy resale flats, but they're generally excluded from new-flat schemes such as BTO, Sale of Balance Flats (SBF), and 3Gen flats unless the Citizen is the sole or primary applicant.

### Non-Citizen Household Members

Two Permanent Residents alone — with no Singapore Citizen in the household — can still qualify under specific schemes, provided each has held PR status for three years or more. This is Singapore PR HDB eligibility at its narrowest: it works, but only once both PRs clear the tenure bar.

A Singapore Citizen may also include a non-citizen, non-PR spouse in the household as an occupier — not a co-owner — under the Non-Citizen Spouse Scheme. We cover the full mechanics of that scheme, including flat-type restrictions, in the Special Eligibility Schemes section below.

A household with no Singapore Citizen or Permanent Resident member at all doesn't fit any of the configurations above. This is the one hard boundary in an otherwise flexible system: non-citizen HDB eligibility only ever exists through a citizen or PR household member, never on its own.

## 2. How to Check Your Citizenship or PR Status Before You Apply

Here's the thing: most guides to HDB eligibility jump straight into the rules and assume you already know your own citizenship or PR status. In practice, that's not always obvious — especially if you've recently changed status, or you're checking on behalf of a family member. Before you start an HFE letter application, confirm your status through Singpass or the ICA's MyICA e-service; application and status updates arrive by email once an outcome is ready, so you're not left guessing.

This step matters more than it looks. A citizenship or PR-status change for any applicant or occupier — after your HFE letter has already been approved — invalidates that letter. The household then has to cancel it and apply for a fresh one before submitting a new flat or resale flat application.

Think of this as a prerequisite check, not a scheme choice. The scheme itself is determined by the citizenship categories covered throughout the rest of this article — this step just makes sure the categories you're checking against are accurate.

## 3. Permanent Resident Eligibility: The 3-Year SPR Tenure Rule

<!-- info-gain anchor: angle 2 -->

Here's a rule that trips up more PR households than it should: a Permanent Resident applicant, and any PR members of the family nucleus, must have held SPR status for at least three years at the time of the HFE letter or resale application ([HDB Couples and Families eligibility](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/buying-a-flat/flat-grant-and-loan-eligibility/couples-and-families)).

Don't confuse this with the 30-month wait-out period some resale sellers face — that's a separate, unrelated condition tied to private property disposal, not PR tenure. The 3-year SPR tenure rule and the 30-month wait-out period are independent checks, and conflating them is a common and costly mistake.

The practical effect is straightforward: a PR household member who hasn't yet reached the three-year mark simply can't be counted toward eligibility. For a household counting on that PR's tenure, this can delay the purchase timeline by months, sometimes longer. This is the crux of PR 3-year wait HDB resale flat planning — track how long each PR member has held that status, and plan your application timeline around whichever member reaches three years last.

If you're a PR nearing eligibility on both fronts, it's worth weighing your options. Applying for Singapore Citizenship requires only two years' PR tenure (and age 21 or above) ([Singapore Citizenship eligibility (ICA)](https://www.ica.gov.sg/reside/citizenship)), which is shorter than the 3-year HDB threshold. Depending on your circumstances, applying for citizenship might get you to eligibility faster than simply waiting out the HDB tenure clock — though citizenship is a bigger decision than a flat purchase timeline alone should drive.

## 4. Resale Flat Eligibility: Ethnic Integration Policy and SPR Quota

<!-- info-gain anchor: angle 1 -->

Clearing the citizenship gate doesn't automatically clear you to buy any resale flat you like. Resale purchases face two further checks that sit on top of citizenship status, and neither gets much attention in most HDB eligibility guides.

The first is the Ethnic Integration Policy (EIP). Resale purchases are capped by ethnic-group quotas at both the block and neighbourhood level, and HDB checks this via its e-Service before your resale purchase can proceed ([Ethnic Integration Policy and SPR Quota](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/buying-a-flat/resale-flats/process-for-buying-a-resale-flat/resale-flat-planning/eip-and-spr-quota)). If a block or neighbourhood has already hit its quota for your ethnic group, you won't be able to buy there — regardless of how strong your citizenship eligibility otherwise is.

The second is the SPR quota. Non-Malaysian SPR households face an additional, separate cap: 8% at the block level and 5% at the neighbourhood level. This applies specifically to non-Malaysian Permanent Resident buyers, on top of — not instead of — the ethnic quota above.

It's worth being clear about how these two gates interact with everything covered so far: a household can fully meet the SC/PR citizenship rules from Section 1 and the 3-year SPR tenure rule from Section 3, and still be blocked from a specific block because of EIP or SPR quota limits. Clearing citizenship status HDB eligibility gets you in the door; EIP and SPR quota decide which specific units are actually open to you. This is also the direct answer to a question a lot of buyers search for but rarely find addressed: yes, ethnic eligibility requirements are real, and they apply independently of your citizenship status.

## 5. Special Eligibility Schemes

The Non-Citizen Spouse Scheme deserves a closer look, since it's the main route for a mixed-citizenship couple where one spouse hasn't taken up citizenship or PR status. Under this scheme, a Singapore Citizen may buy a flat together with a non-citizen, non-PR spouse — but that spouse is listed as an occupier, not a co-owner, which affects what they can and can't do with the flat later.

There's also a flat-type restriction to know about. BTO purchases under this scheme are limited to a 2-Room Flexi flat. Resale purchases have more flexibility — any flat type or size on the open market qualifies, except 3Gen flats, which are reserved for multi-generation family nuclei.

This is a summary-level look at a scheme with real nuance, worked examples, and edge cases that go well beyond what fits here. If a non-citizen spouse is part of your household picture, our dedicated guide to the Non-Citizen Spouse Scheme covers the full mechanics.

## 6. Additional Buyer Categories

Beyond the core citizenship configurations, HDB recognizes several multi-generation and extended-family buyer categories — including priority-scheme applicants who buy alongside parents or married children. These categories exist because household structures in Singapore don't always fit the standard couple-or-single mould, and HDB has built pathways for them.

It's worth being clear that these categories layer additional family-nucleus and income conditions on top of the base citizenship gate covered in Section 1 — they don't replace it. A multi-generation household still needs to satisfy the underlying SC, SC+SPR, or SC+non-citizen configuration before the category-specific rules even come into play.

The detailed criteria and worked scenarios for these categories are extensive enough to warrant their own treatment. Our guide to multi-generation family eligibility covers them in full.

## 7. Financial Considerations

Meeting citizenship status HDB eligibility requirements is necessary, but it doesn't guarantee grant or loan eligibility on its own. Income ceilings vary by scheme and by household composition, and a household that clears every citizenship rule in this article can still find its grant options narrower than expected once income comes into the picture.

PR-inclusive households, in particular, typically face more limited grant access compared to SC-only households. It's a distinction worth planning around early, rather than discovering it after you've settled on a flat type and scheme.

## 8. FAQ

### Can non-citizens buy HDB?

Not on their own. Every eligible household configuration requires at least one Singapore Citizen or Permanent Resident member. A non-citizen can be part of an eligible purchase only as the spouse of a Singapore Citizen, listed as an occupier rather than a co-owner, under the Non-Citizen Spouse Scheme.

### How to check citizenship status in Singapore?

You can check your citizenship or PR status, and any pending application status, through Singpass or the ICA's MyICA e-service. Once an outcome is available, ICA sends an email notification, so you don't need to keep checking manually.

### What are the ethnic eligibility requirements for HDB flats in Singapore?

Resale flat purchases are subject to the Ethnic Integration Policy, which caps buyers by ethnic group at both the block and neighbourhood level. Non-Malaysian SPR households face an additional SPR quota — 8% at the block level and 5% at the neighbourhood level — on top of the ethnic quota. Both checks run through HDB's e-Service before a resale purchase can proceed.

### Is there a "Non-Citizen Family Scheme" for HDB flat buyers?

Not under that exact name. The relevant pathway is the Non-Citizen Spouse Scheme, which lets a Singapore Citizen buy with a non-citizen, non-PR spouse as an occupier. BTO purchases under this scheme are limited to a 2-Room Flexi flat, while resale purchases allow any flat type except 3Gen flats.

## Conclusion

Citizenship status HDB eligibility is the first gate you'll clear, and it comes down to one of three configurations: a Singapore Citizen household, an SC plus SPR household, or an SC plus non-citizen spouse. But it's not the only gate. The 3-year PR tenure rule, and — for resale purchases — the Ethnic Integration Policy and SPR quota, are separate, additional checks that can block an otherwise-eligible household. Clearing Section 1 doesn't mean you've cleared everything; it means you're through the first door.
